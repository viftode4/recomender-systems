# What the selected predictions actually changed

The categorical reconstruction improved mean development nDCG@10 from 0.261688 to 0.263154, but it did so by making small adjustments to nearly identical rankings. Eligible-score Pearson correlation is 0.99932, and mean top-10 overlap is 97.81%. Per seed, the overlap is 97.18%, 96.25%, and 100%; the third seed selected the binary fallback. These measurements concern the selected configurations, not every possible use of categorical ratings.

At top 10, the categorical model gains 24, 29, and 0 validation positives that the binary model misses, while losing 8, 20, and 0 binary hits. It recovers only 0.62%, 0.77%, and 0% of the binary model's missed positives. Its gains do not extend uniformly deeper into the catalog: mean positive rank changes from 138.6 to 139.0, and macro recall@100 is 0.7167 versus 0.7162. Top-10 nDCG can improve while deeper ranking slightly worsens; there is no contradiction between those endpoints.

Both models overwhelmingly recommend popular items. The head is the top 337 of 1,682 catalog items by original TRAIN frequency. It receives about 98.9% of their recommendation slots. Tail items account for 33.3%–34.8% of validation positive pairs, but categorical pooled recall@10 on these items is 0.37%, 0.66%, and 0.69%, compared with roughly 27% for head items. Tail positives contribute 40%–42% of all misses. This identifies a concrete deficiency; it does not establish that popularity correction would improve the original accuracy objective. Tail recommendations can trade successful head hits for poorly supported candidates, so any correction needs the same untouched selection discipline and a matched objective/control.

Sparse users are not where most missed pairs reside. Dense users account for about 68% of positive pairs and 71% of missed pairs. They also receive the highest average nDCG, while their pooled recall is lower because a fixed list of ten must cover many more positives. Claims about a particular activity group therefore depend on the metric and its denominator; “dense users cause most errors” would be misleading without those counts.

SLIM has substantially different rankings: roughly 66% top-10 overlap with binary reconstruction. It recovers about 5.1%–5.6% of binary misses, compared with less than 0.8% for categories in the two non-fallback seeds. Nevertheless, most positives are missed by both. A truth-informed per-user choice between binary and SLIM would improve mean nDCG by 0.04070 over the better fixed expert, but this is an unattainable oracle statistic. It neither supplies a deployable selection rule nor proves that training history contains enough information to predict the better expert. The already selected two-expert hybrid achieves 0.265593; adding categories changes that to only 0.265666.

The evidence supports a narrower conclusion than “we need a larger architecture” or “we need more data”: the tested categorical residual representation plus the selected reconstruction objective produced little new ranking information. Other-user sample size, missing context, model capacity, loss weighting, and irreducible uncertainty remain distinct hypotheses. The fixed-query donor sensitivity tests only the first under a controlled subset of this dataset. A separate nonlinear interaction study can test whether a genuinely different representation changes the rankings and error pattern, but its benefit must be measured rather than inferred from its complexity.

These are post-final-test exploratory observations on the reused 472-user development cohorts. The three seeds reuse MovieLens records, not independent populations. They supply no fresh confirmation, chronological claim, information-theoretic ceiling, or evidence about MovieLens data outside the observed experiment.

Evidence: `results-v1/aggregates.json`, `results-v1/provenance.json`, and `results-v1/SHA256.json`. The read-only calculation took 6.53 seconds, validated the original completion/source/payload hash chain twice, and independently reproduced all 24 selected model/seed nDCG values within 2e-12. Five focused diagnostic tests and an independent review checked masks, stable ties, conditional denominators, oracle interpretation, and aggregate-only outputs. # Safe implementation reuse for a new study

Use the sealed `exploratory/categorical_reconstruction/run_experiment.py` TRAIN loader or the completed study's hash-verified `training-categories.npz`, original `cohorts.json`, and validation pair IDs. Preserve the same ordered 943-user/1,683-column score shape, padding column, original TRAIN candidate exclusion, and 471/472 meta/development split. New model outputs must be finite before masking. Reuse pure ranking and metric helpers with identical endpoint definitions.

Create a new runner, explicit candidate grid, protocol, source/input/runtime seals, and a global selection barrier for every new family. Do not monkeypatch the prior runner's hardcoded candidate families or rewrite old selections. No RecBole split regeneration is needed. All development choices remain exploratory because these cohorts have already informed research directions.

For a weighting proposal, state the objective algebra before fitting. Multiplying one entire target column's squared-error term by a constant weight is equivalent to changing that target's effective ridge penalty; scaling only target values can merely rescale its prediction column. Those are useful controls, but neither alone establishes a new learned interaction. Entry-dependent weights, genuinely context-dependent features, or a different likelihood impose different assumptions and require explicit matched comparisons.
